# 0.1.0 performance

Measured performance, memory, incremental and query baseline of grammar
version 0.1.0 (Session 05, 2026-09-23/24 UTC; recovery complexity was
investigated, three families fixed in full or in memory and the release
parser re-measured in Session 05-1, 2026-09-24). It is a release baseline, not a speed claim: it
states what was measured on one machine. One limitation is disclosed, as a
class: [KL-002](#known-limitation-kl-002), quadratic error-recovery time with
small memory on long runs of malformed pieces inside right-recursive
nestings. Fixed:
[unclosed calls and indexes](#fixed-in-session-05-1-unclosed-calls-and-indexes-r-a-01),
[the memory of the `^` family](#fixed-in-session-05-1-memory-of-the--family-formerly-kl-003)
and [the memory of PRINT items](#fixed-in-session-05-1-memory-of-print-items-b4-01-b4-02).
Every number is an empirical observation for the inputs and machine below,
not a guarantee. Raw samples, inputs and scripts are local evidence:
`artifacts/session-05-final-release-freeze/04-performance/` (identity
`EVIDENCE-SHA256SUMS` = `0fa05e41965332762963d83d359ea1b36ee5e497b91bdd63b75a281a822b290d`)
and `artifacts/session-05-1-b01-recovery-complexity/` (root cause
`EVIDENCE-ROOT-CAUSE-SHA256SUMS` = `af400263fcbfde96fb0df4660dc29c97be8599bbc99b1b6918338a60a14e2449`;
re-audit `16-reaudit/EVIDENCE-REAUDIT-SHA256SUMS` = `d55d6b59afca6a5b8ea4cb2c836921d1ffb0cc414dd1cd639be6d8725cbe3b7f`;

below 200 KB, 10 up to 2 MB and 5 above; one parser object, a fresh parse

with no error, all malformed inputs with one.

## Known limitation KL-002

A class of behaviour, found in steps: the Session 05 final review (B-01,
prefix operators), the Session 05-1 re-audits (B2-02: `not`; B2-01: `^`;
B3-01 and B3-03: nested single-line IFs and continuation across lines) and
its delta re-audit (B4-01, B4-02: the PRINT change below trades memory for
time on PRINT continued by malformed lines); record in
[validation.md](../validation/validation.md) (KL-002).

**Class.** Error recovery can take time quadratic in the number of repeated
malformed pieces that keep a right-recursive nesting pending in one statement
or its continuation. Measured families, all on the release parser:

| Family | Example | Pinned CLI (`--time`, median of 3) | Native |
|---|---|---|---|
| prefix operator + forcing token | `x = ` + `+*`×k | 2 KB 0.38 s; 4 KB 1.52 s; 8 KB 6.37 s | 16 KB 22.9 s |
| the same across lines | `x = -⏎` + `-⏎`×k | 4 KB 1.62 s; 8 KB 6.61 s | 4 KB 1.69 s * |
| `^` + non-operand | `x = ` + `2^*`×k | 6 KB 1.62 s; 12 KB 6.83 s; 24 KB 29.8 s | 6 KB 1.31 s |
| nested single-line IF, condition across a line | `if a⏎*2`×k | 7 KB 0.55 s; 14 KB 2.13 s; 28 KB 9.00 s | 14 KB 2.27 s * |
| directive inside an expression | `x = ` + `#if a⏎*2`×k | — | 16 KB 2.12 s * |
| PRINT continued by malformed lines | `print ` + `,+⏎`×k | 1.5 KB 0.76 s; 3 KB 3.05 s; 6 KB 12.9 s | 6 KB 11.1 s |

\* From the family sweep, run with four parallel workers (slower than a
single run).

Also quadratic by the same mechanism: `x = (not)not)…`, `+`, `-` or `not`
followed by `/`, `,`, `)` or `<`, inside closed `(…)` and `[…]`, in call
arguments across lines, after `print`, `return`, `if`, `while`, `for` and
`dim`, `2^)`, `2^,`, `2^-*`, `^` runs inside closed groups (`x = (2^*…)`, linear
before the `_pow_left` change), `x = if a then⏎*2`, `x = #if a⏎if a`, `#if`
inside `print`, `(` or `f(`, and other malformed lines after an unfinished
PRINT (`print ;-⏎`; 13 such families in the 12,115-family sweep below, the
slowest `print ,+⏎`). The native witness `x = ` + `+*`×k takes 21.1 ms,
80.1 ms, 316 ms, 1.25 s, 5.21 s and 22.9 s at k = 250 … 8,000 (2 KB steps of
×2; exponent 1.9–2.1). The list is not exhaustive: other right-recursive
nestings may behave the same. The round-5 re-audit found PRINT continued not
only by lines but also by `:`, `else`, labels or directives (64 of 8,000 unit
triples, 225 of 8,649 pairs). Memory stays small in the measured families: at
most 13.5 MiB native for the prefix witness at 16 KB and about 24 MiB for the
CLI process (`^`, 24 KB); the open findings B5-01 and B5-02
([0.1.0-release.md](0.1.0-release.md#status-hold)) are not covered. Not
affected (linear): valid input of the same size (`x = 1` followed by `+1`×8,000:
5.4 ms; by `+-1`×8,000: 9.2 ms), `*/` or `not *` repeated, block statements,
`if a⏎)` and `if a⏎.b`, statements separated by valid lines, and unfinished
PRINT lines among valid lines (100 of them in a 120 KB program: 24 ms; valid:
19 ms).

**Runtime and generator.** On the release parser the prefix, `^`, nested-IF
and PRINT-across-lines witnesses give identical trees and quadratic curves
with runtimes 0.25.1, 0.26.13 and 0.27.0 (the PRINT witness is about 2.8×
faster on 0.25.1), and generators 0.26.13 and 0.27.0 produce byte-identical
`src/` files from this grammar. On `fec46d21…` the prefix curve was also
unchanged after reverting any one of the 0.27.0 recovery commits `869638f6`
(upstream issue #5910, a recovery tree-shape regression), `f30ee2b3` or
`15ea3328`. Neither a pin change nor a runtime choice removes the class, and
this repository does not ship a runtime.

**Cause.** Each malformed piece makes the runtime recover to the shallowest
earlier state that accepts the next piece, which is the state inside the
previous nesting level (after a prefix operator, a `^`, inside a single-line
IF branch, or inside the right-recursive PRINT item list), because the grammar
lets that construct nest. The pending levels therefore grow by one per piece,
and the version that inserted a missing operand reduces the whole pending
chain again at the next forcing token: the parse performs about k² reductions
(1.000·k² for the prefix witness, 1.5·k² for the nested IF witness) at
constant cost each, while every other recovery event is linear. 13-rule
grammars with only prefix `+`/`-`, or only a right-associative `^`, show the
same curves. Forbidding the nesting makes each family linear (checked for
prefix operators and for single-line IFs) but rejects syntax 0.1.0 accepts
(BS-EXP-028, BS-STMT-009); lowering the prefix precedence contradicts
BS-EXP-012 and leaves `-)` quadratic; bounding the nesting depth would also
change the language (not tried); a flat prefix-run encoding keeps the language
and is linear in the minimal grammar, but changes the public shape of
`unary_expression`, which the owner declined for 0.1.0; for PRINT, the
`repeat()` list that avoids the pending chain needs quadratic memory instead
(below). No grammar change was found that keeps both the accepted language and
the tree. The legacy grammar is linear on all these families: it has no
prefix `+`/`-`, no `^`, no nested single-line IF and no PRINT juxtaposition.

**Realistic editing.** Scattered errors stay at valid-input cost: one 169 KB
expression with 1, 10 or 100 `+ *` sites parses in 17.5–20.6 ms (valid:
17.4 ms); a 120 KB program with 100 sites in 23.1 ms (valid: 18.8 ms); a
99 KB multi-line array with 100 malformed elements in 21.2 ms (valid:
15.3 ms). Inserting `* ` into the long expression and removing it again
reparse incrementally in 3.9 and 4.2 ms (fresh: 17 ms), in the program in 0.8
and 0.8 ms (fresh: 20 ms); each incremental tree equals the fresh one. The
class needs hundreds of consecutive malformed pieces.

**Mitigation.** A consumer that parses untrusted or generated text can bound
the parse time with the runtime's progress callback
(`ts_parser_parse_with_options`, `TSParseOptions.progress_callback`,
available in 0.25–0.27): a callback that returns true after 200 ms stopped the
16 KB prefix witness, the 24 KB `^` witness, the 28 KB nested-IF witness and
the 6 KB PRINT witness at 200.0 ms. Editors that reparse incrementally are
affected only while such a run exists.

**Release impact.** 0.1.0 ships with KL-002 disclosed as a class. Its W13
witnesses and its four rows of the recovery scaling guards
([validation.md](../validation/validation.md)) run locally and in hosted CI;
a guard fails if its curve becomes worse than quadratic, its larger parse
exceeds its time bound (3–5 s) or its memory grows by more than 24–32 MiB
(validation.md states the floor of the Linux memory reading).

## Fixed in Session 05-1: unclosed calls and indexes (R-A-01)

Found by the Session 05-1 re-audit (Reviewer A R-A-01, Reviewer B B2-01). A
run of unclosed calls or indexes, each opened with a token that cannot start
its content, in one expression or statement (`x = f(*f(*…`, `a[*`, `f(=`,
`a.b(*`, `a?.b(*`, `a@b(*`, `f(⏎*`, statement `f(=`), took quadratic time and
memory before the fix. Native, runtime 0.27.0, k = 2,000:

| Input | Bytes | Before (`fec46d21…`) | After |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `x = ` + `f(*`×k | 6,005 | 2.69 s, 1,269 MiB | 6.1 ms, 7.5 MiB (release parser) |
| `x = ` + `a[*`×k | 6,005 | 2.84 s, 1,269 MiB | 6.0 ms, 7.5 MiB (release parser) |
| `f(=`×k (statement) | 6,001 | 3.69 s, 1,472 MiB | 9.2 ms, 5.3 MiB (`6da8a470…`) |
| `x = ` + `a.b(*`×k | 10,005 | 3.50 s | 10.3 ms, 8.4 MiB (`6da8a470…`) |

Before the fix the native builds on runtimes 0.25.1, 0.26.13 and 0.27.0 gave
the same 1,269 MiB, and the pinned CLI (runtime 0.27.0) needed 1.24 GiB at
6 KB, a `FAIL` of W06's memory bound; the legacy grammar and
tree-sitter-python were linear on the same input (36 and 14 ms). Cause: at end
of input the runtime accepts the error version by popping every path of its
stack (`ts_parser__accept` → `ts_stack_pop_all`), copying a subtree array per
path; with the postfix operand inlined, recovery left a stack one level deeper
per repetition and merged at every level, so the copies grew quadratically and
stayed allocated until the parse ended. No progress callback runs in that
step. A 13-rule grammar with calls reproduces it when the call operand is
inlined and is linear when it is not. The fix keeps `src/node-types.json` and
every valid tree. Its row of the recovery scaling guards is a regression test
(exponent ≤ 1.5, ≤ 300 ms, ≤ 24 MiB growth; the unfixed parser fails all
three).

## Fixed in Session 05-1: memory of the `^` family (formerly KL-003)

Found by the Session 05-1 re-audit (Reviewer B B2-01); first disclosed as
KL-003 with an owner exception to the bounded-memory condition, then fixed
after the final re-audit (B3-02) showed a tree-preserving change. Before
(`6da8a470…`): `x = ` + `2^*`×k needed 83 MiB native at 6 KB and 1,137 MiB at
24 KB (the CLI process 1.10 GiB), by the same end-of-input mechanism as
R-A-01, and a 200 ms progress callback stopped the 24 KB input only after
1,966 ms. After (`3f3eafd1…`, the parser of that change): 8.7 MiB native at
6 KB and 18 MiB at 24 KB, and the callback stops the 24 KB input at 200.1 ms;
the release parser gives 8.8 MiB at 6 KB, 23.7 MiB for the CLI process at
24 KB and a stop at 200.0 ms. The time is quadratic and part of KL-002 (6 KB:
1.31 s native, 1.62 s through the CLI; before: 0.16 s). The change wraps the
left operand of `^` in a hidden rule with `POSTFIX` precedence (grammar-design
§5); `2^3^2` is still `2^(3^2)` and `-2^2` still `-(2^2)`, and no valid tree
changed on 2,452 inputs. The owner's memory exception is withdrawn and KL-003
is retired. The guard row "KL-002 exponent" is also a regression test of this
fix (memory growth ≤ 24 MiB; before: 69–84 MiB).

## Fixed in Session 05-1: memory of PRINT items (B4-01, B4-02)

Found by the Session 05-1 delta re-audit (Reviewer B4). Runs of malformed PRINT
items, such as unclosed calls with a stray `)` (`print f([)f([)…`) or a
separator followed by a prefix operator and a forcing token (`print ,+*,+*…`),
needed quadratic memory at end of input that a progress callback could not
interrupt; the parsers since Session 05 behaved alike. Native, runtime 0.27.0:

| Input | Bytes | Before (`3f3eafd1…`) | After (release parser) | Legacy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `print ` + `f([)`×8,000 | 32,007 | 2.05 s, 1,466 MiB | 78 ms, 31.2 MiB | 62 ms, 13.9 MiB |
| `print ` + `,+*`×8,000 | 24,007 | 1.05 s, 747 MiB | 123 ms, 27.3 MiB | 13 ms, 9.6 MiB |

Before the change the pinned CLI needed 1,474 MiB for the 32 KB input, a
`FAIL` of W06's memory bound, and a 200 ms callback returned only after
2,076 ms; after it the CLI needs 36.6 MiB and the parse ends in 0.08 s. The
same unit after `x = ` was linear before (32 KB: 16 MiB): the cost came from
the `repeat()` item list, whose recovery left a deep merged stack for the
end-of-input acceptance described under R-A-01. Item wrappers, inlining,
precedence variants and a left-recursive list changed nothing; the
right-recursive hidden list `_print_items` (grammar-design §6) removes the
memory, keeps `src/node-types.json` and every valid tree (75,613 inputs), and
costs quadratic time on PRINT continued by malformed lines (KL-002 table;
before: 6 KB 0.12 s native, 57 MiB). A sweep of 12,115 families (the sweeps
below and the delta re-audit's family, pair and random-unit sets) on both
parsers: on the release parser no family grows by more than 16 MiB between
its two sizes and none exceeds 22 MiB (before: up to 148 MiB); the 13 families
whose time became superlinear are all PRINT continued by malformed lines. The
guard row "B4-01 PRINT items" is a regression test of this fix (exponent
≤ 1.5, ≤ 300 ms, ≤ 24 MiB growth).

## Release gate and interpretation

- Gate (validation.md V10, workloads W06 and W13): every W06 input and W13
  seed within 10 s and 1 GiB on the release parser, the KL-002, R-A-01 and
  PRINT witnesses included; the largest valid input measured (5 MB) needs
  0.69 s and 161 MiB. Larger KL-002 inputs exceed the time bound;
  validation.md "KL-002 and the V10 bound" states how this is recorded.
- Until the round-5 re-audit of `e6a5a7c` no crash or hang was observed and
  no measured family needed more than small memory on the release parser.
  That re-audit found families with quadratic memory (B5-01), a stack
  overflow on long malformed PRINT runs (B5-02) and a quadratic highlight
  query within long PRINT statements (A5-01), all open
  ([0.1.0-release.md](0.1.0-release.md#status-hold)). Otherwise growth is linear on valid input and on
  every malformed family measured except the KL-002 class, which is quadratic
  in time with small memory. The recovery sweep of 157 malformed families
  (k = 500 and 2,000) peaks at 15.0 MiB; the reviewers of the re-audits found
  no quadratic-time family outside the class text in their sweeps. The call
  and index family and the memory of the `^` and PRINT families were fixed in
  Session 05-1.
- No optimisation was made beyond those fixes. No profile was retained for the
  Session 05 measurements; the Session 05-1 causes were isolated with
  parse-event counts, an instrumented runtime, a generator and runtime matrix,
  minimal grammars and grammar spikes.
- The slowest measured families belong to KL-002 (PRINT continued by
  malformed lines passes 10 s near 5 KB through the CLI). Apart from them, the
  slowest case is malformed input of an unresolved form (bare CR line endings,
  0.4 MiB/s on the release parser, linear).
- Limitations: one machine and one OS; Linux performance is only indirectly
  observed (CI W06 and guard timings); the harness is not shipped; the
  comparison with the legacy grammar covers only syntax both accept; the
  KL-002 families are those found, not all that may exist.
